The problem

Money apps ask for your bank login, then hand you a dashboard that feels like homework. Most people stop looking.

CashGlome starts from the other end. There's no bank connection to break and nothing to set up, and the reason to come back each month has nothing to do with guilt.

Writing

Many entries, one line.

“bolt 6k, lunch 4.5k, got paid 350k” becomes three entries, each in the right category, and you see what it understood before anything is saved. It reads slang, pidgin and shorthand like k and m, in 15 currencies.

Say it

Hold the microphone and talk. What you say is read the same way.

Snap it

Photograph a receipt. It's read on your device, and the photo stays there.

Repeat it

What we believe

Counted, not guessed

The AI plans and explains. The arithmetic happens on your device, so the numbers are exact.

Questions

Asked often.

Is it free?

Yes. Everything that runs on your device is free, for good. A paid plan with hosted AI and sync is planned for people who want it.

Do I need to connect my bank?

No. You write, say or snap what happened. There's no bank login to give and no sync to break.

Where is my data?

On your device. There's no CashGlome account and no server holding your entries. You can export everything as a file whenever you like.

What does the AI see?

Nothing, unless you switch on Muse and say yes. Then it sees your question, your monthly totals and the entries each search finds: names, amounts, dates and categories. Never photos. The adding up happens on your device.

Which currencies and languages?

Fifteen currencies, from the naira and shilling to the dollar, pound, euro and yen. It reads English, including everyday slang and Nigerian pidgin, with more languages to come.

Is this financial advice?

No. CashGlome shows what you've written down and answers questions about it. For decisions about investing or debt, talk to a licensed adviser.

Why paintings?

Because people come back to things that are beautiful and kind, not to dashboards that scold. Every painting is a public-domain masterpiece.
